# Pre-Medicare Participants, ACA Plans and Subsidies

Some sponsors extend their retiree program to people who retire before 65. These participants are not yet Medicare eligible and buy an individual plan under the Affordable Care Act (ACA) through Benefits Marketplace. This document covers plan basics, the enrollment calendar, financial help, and what changes at 65.

## Metal tiers

ACA individual plans are grouped by how much of the average person's costs the plan pays:

- Bronze: lowest monthly premium, highest deductible. Many Bronze plans are HSA-compatible.
- Silver: moderate premium and deductible. Silver is the only tier where cost-sharing reductions apply.
- Gold: higher premium, low deductible and predictable copays.

Every tier covers the same essential health benefits, including prescription drugs, preventive care, hospital care and mental health services. Plans differ in network type (HMO, EPO or PPO), formulary tiers and service area. Utah plans are approved by county.

## Enrollment calendar

Open Enrollment for ACA plans runs from November 1 through January 15 each year. An application submitted by December 15 starts coverage on January 1. Applications between December 16 and January 15 start on February 1.

Outside Open Enrollment, a participant needs a Special Enrollment Period (SEP) and generally has 60 days from the qualifying event to enroll. Common qualifying events:

- Losing other coverage, such as an employer plan, COBRA running out, or losing Medicaid.
- Moving to a new county or state where different plans are offered.
- Marriage, divorce with loss of coverage, birth or adoption of a child.
- Death of the person who held the coverage.
- A change in household income that changes eligibility for financial help.

Agents describe SEPs as possible and route the participant to a licensed benefit advisor, who confirms the event, the dates and the documentation the Marketplace will ask for.

## Premium tax credits and cost-sharing reductions

The federal Marketplace may lower the monthly premium with a premium tax credit based on household income and family size. Silver plans may also carry cost-sharing reductions that lower deductibles and copays for people who qualify.

Sponsor HRA funds and premium tax credits interact. Depending on how the sponsor's program is structured, being offered or accepting HRA reimbursement for premiums can reduce or remove eligibility for the tax credit. Agents state that the interaction exists, do not estimate any tax credit amount, and do not give tax advice. A licensed benefit advisor walks through the options, and participants may also want to speak with a tax professional.

## Turning 65: the transition to Medicare

A pre-Medicare participant should plan the move to Medicare four to six months before their 65th birthday. The main points:

- The Medicare Initial Enrollment Period starts three months before the birthday month. See [KB-MKT-001].
- Premium tax credits end once the person is eligible for premium-free Part A, so most people end the ACA plan the day Medicare coverage begins. Keeping both means paying for coverage that no longer works together.
- The participant chooses between Medicare Advantage or Original Medicare with Medigap and Part D. See [KB-MKT-002].
- Sponsor HRA rules may change at 65. Some sponsors fund the HRA only after Medicare Part B is effective; others fund pre-65 and post-65 retirees at different amounts. Agents read the sponsor record and explain what it says.
- Medigap open enrollment starts when the person is 65 and has Part B, and lasts six months.

## How agents use this document

Explain the calendar and the tiers, confirm the participant's county, and use the plan search tool to list plans side by side. When a participant asks which tier or plan to choose, or how much tax credit they would get, say a licensed benefit advisor will help and offer the handoff. Cite this document when you describe dates, tiers or the transition at 65.
