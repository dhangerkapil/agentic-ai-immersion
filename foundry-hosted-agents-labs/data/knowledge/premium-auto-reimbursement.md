# Premium Auto-Reimbursement

Premium auto-reimbursement pays a participant back for a monthly plan premium without a claim form each month. Once set up, Benefits Marketplace receives payment confirmation from the carrier and releases the reimbursement from the HRA automatically. This document explains who qualifies, how to enroll, timing, and what to check when a payment stops.

## Which premiums qualify

Automatic reimbursement is available for premiums on plans the participant enrolled in through Benefits Marketplace, where the carrier shares monthly payment data. That includes most Medicare Advantage, Medigap, Part D, and dental or vision plans on the marketplace, subject to the sponsor's eligible expense types. Plans purchased outside Benefits Marketplace are reimbursed through regular claims, because no carrier data feed exists.

The Medicare Part B premium is handled by a related feature called recurring premium reimbursement. The participant submits proof of the Part B amount once a year, usually the Social Security benefit statement or Form SSA-1099, and the monthly amount is reimbursed automatically for the rest of the year.

ACA individual plan premiums can be reimbursed automatically for some carriers. When the carrier does not report payments, the participant files monthly or quarterly claims with the carrier billing statement and proof of payment. See [KB-ACC-001].

## How to enroll

The participant turns on auto-reimbursement in the portal under account settings, or asks a benefit advisor to enable it during enrollment. A one-time authorization confirms that reimbursements may be sent by direct deposit to the bank account on file. Without a bank account on file, payments arrive by check and take longer.

## Timing

Carriers report a premium as paid after they receive it. Reimbursement is released within three business days of that report. For most participants the deposit arrives between the fifth and the fifteenth of the month. The first payment after enrollment or after a plan change can take four to six weeks while the carrier feed is matched to the account. Reimbursements stop when the HRA balance reaches zero and resume when the new plan year is funded.

## Plan changes and January

When a participant changes plans during the Annual Enrollment Period, the auto-reimbursement setup must be confirmed for the new plan. The premium amount is updated each January when carriers publish new rates. No new form is needed for a rate change on the same plan.

## When a reimbursement stops

Check these causes in order:

1. The HRA balance is exhausted for the plan year.
2. The carrier has not reported the payment, often because the participant's own payment was late or returned.
3. The plan was terminated or replaced and the new plan is not linked.
4. The direct deposit failed because the bank account changed.
5. The sponsor changed eligible expense types for the new plan year.

Agents read the account record for the balance, the enabled flag and recent claim status, then explain the most likely cause. If the carrier feed appears to be missing, the participant can file a regular claim for that month so the reimbursement is not delayed further.

## Turning it off

Auto-reimbursement can be turned off at any time in the portal. Participants who prefer to pay premiums with the HRA debit card should turn it off first, since paying with the card and receiving an automatic reimbursement for the same premium creates a duplicate that has to be repaid. See [KB-ACC-002].

## What agents may say

Confirm whether auto-reimbursement is enabled, state the balance, explain timing and the checklist above, and describe how to enable or disable it. Agents do not change account settings or bank details in this workshop. Route bank account changes and disputed amounts to a benefit advisor. Cite this document.
