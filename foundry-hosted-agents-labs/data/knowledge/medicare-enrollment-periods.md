# Medicare Enrollment Periods

This document explains when a Medicare-eligible participant can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage, Part D or Medigap plan. Agents use it to state windows and deadlines. A licensed benefit advisor confirms eligibility for any Special Enrollment Period and completes every enrollment.

## Initial Enrollment Period (IEP)

The IEP is a seven-month window around the month a person turns 65: the three months before the birthday month, the birthday month, and the three months after. Someone with a November birthday has an IEP from August 1 through the last day of February. If the birthday falls on the first day of a month, Medicare treats the previous month as the birthday month and the whole window moves one month earlier.

During the IEP a participant can enroll in Part A and Part B, choose a Medicare Advantage or Part D plan, and buy a Medigap policy. Coverage usually starts on the first day of the birthday month when the application arrives during the three months before it. Later applications start the month after the application.

## Medigap Open Enrollment

Separate from the IEP, a person has a six-month Medigap open enrollment that begins the month they are both 65 or older and enrolled in Part B. During these six months carriers must accept the application without health questions. After the window closes, carriers in most states, including Utah, may underwrite and can decline or charge more.

## Annual Enrollment Period (AEP): October 15 to December 7

Every year, anyone with Medicare can join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage plan or a Part D plan, or move from Medicare Advantage back to Original Medicare and add a Part D plan. Changes take effect on January 1. Because sponsor HRA plan years also start on January 1, AEP is the busiest season for Benefits Marketplace and the period when most comparison questions arrive.

## Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment Period (OEP): January 1 to March 31

Only people already enrolled in a Medicare Advantage plan can use the OEP. They may make one change: switch to a different Medicare Advantage plan, or return to Original Medicare and join a Part D plan. The OEP cannot be used to move from Original Medicare into Medicare Advantage or to switch between two stand-alone Part D plans. Changes take effect the first day of the following month.

## Special Enrollment Periods (SEP)

An SEP lets a participant make a change outside the windows above after a qualifying event. Common triggers:

- Moving outside the current plan's service area, or to a county where new plans are available.
- Losing employer, union or COBRA coverage.
- The plan leaving Medicare or leaving the participant's service area.
- Gaining, losing or changing Medicaid or Extra Help eligibility.
- Moving into, living in, or leaving a nursing home or long-term care facility.
- A one-time switch into a plan rated 5 stars, available December 8 through November 30.

Most SEPs last two months from the qualifying event. The rules differ by trigger, so agents describe the SEP as possible and route the participant to a licensed benefit advisor who confirms the dates and files the enrollment.

## Late enrollment penalties

Delaying Part B or Part D without other creditable coverage can add a permanent penalty to the monthly premium. Agents may say that penalties exist. They must not calculate an amount; Medicare and the advisor determine it.

## How agents use this document

Call the enrollment window tool for the participant, read back the window name and dates, and cite this document. If the participant asks whether they personally qualify for an SEP, say that a licensed benefit advisor will confirm it and offer the handoff.
