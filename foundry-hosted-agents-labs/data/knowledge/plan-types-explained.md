# Plan Types Explained

Benefits Marketplace offers individual plans from more than 80 carriers. Every plan on the marketplace falls into one of the families below. Agents use this document to explain how each family works in plain language. It does not say which family is better for a given person; that judgment belongs to the participant and a licensed benefit advisor.

## Original Medicare (Part A and Part B)

Part A covers hospital stays, skilled nursing after a hospital stay, and hospice. Part B covers doctor visits, outpatient care, equipment and preventive services. Original Medicare has no yearly limit on out-of-pocket costs, generally pays 80 percent of Part B services after the deductible, and does not cover most prescription drugs. Almost every participant adds coverage from one of the families below.

## Medicare Advantage (Part C)

A Medicare Advantage plan is offered by a private carrier and replaces the way a person receives Part A and Part B benefits. Most plans include Part D drug coverage; these are called MA-PD plans. Key features:

- HMO plans use a network and usually require a primary care doctor and referrals. Care outside the network is not covered except in emergencies.
- PPO plans also have a network but cover out-of-network care at a higher cost share, and do not require referrals.
- Every plan sets a maximum out-of-pocket amount for medical services. Once reached, the plan pays 100 percent of covered services for the rest of the year.
- Many plans add dental, vision, hearing, over-the-counter allowances, fitness or transportation benefits.
- Plans are approved by county. A participant must live in the service area.
- CMS rates plans from 1 to 5 stars each year based on quality and member experience.

Premiums range from $0 up. A $0 premium plan still requires the participant to keep paying the Part B premium.

## Medigap (Medicare Supplement)

A Medigap policy works alongside Original Medicare and pays some or all of the cost sharing that Original Medicare leaves behind. Plans are standardized by letter, so Plan G from one carrier covers the same benefits as Plan G from another; only the premium and the carrier's service differ.

- Plan G covers Part A and Part B cost sharing after the participant pays the annual Part B deductible.
- Plan N has a lower premium, requires copays of up to $20 for office visits and up to $50 for emergency visits, and does not cover Part B excess charges.
- Medigap policies have no network. Any doctor who accepts Medicare accepts the policy.
- Medigap does not include drug coverage, so most people pair it with a stand-alone Part D plan.
- Medigap and Medicare Advantage cannot be held at the same time.
- Medigap policies do not receive star ratings.

## Part D (prescription drug plans)

A stand-alone Part D plan, often called a PDP, covers outpatient prescriptions for people on Original Medicare or Medigap. Each plan has a formulary, the list of covered drugs, arranged in tiers. Tier 1 is typically preferred generics with the lowest copay, Tier 2 other generics, Tier 3 preferred brands, Tier 4 non-preferred drugs and Tier 5 specialty drugs. Plans may charge a deductible before coverage starts, often only for higher tiers. Preferred pharmacies and mail order usually cost less. An annual out-of-pocket cap limits what a member pays for covered drugs in a year.

## ACA individual plans (for participants under 65)

Retirees who are not yet Medicare eligible buy individual coverage under the Affordable Care Act. Plans are grouped by metal tier: Bronze, Silver and Gold. See [KB-MKT-003] for open enrollment dates, subsidies and the transition to Medicare at 65.

## How the pieces fit together

A Medicare-eligible participant usually holds one of two combinations: Original Medicare plus a Medigap policy plus a Part D plan, or a single Medicare Advantage plan with drug coverage. A sponsor HRA can reimburse premiums for either combination. See [KB-ACC-001] for reimbursement rules.
