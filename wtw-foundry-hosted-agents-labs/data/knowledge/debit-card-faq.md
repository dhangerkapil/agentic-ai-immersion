# Debit Card FAQ

Sponsors can give participants a Via Benefits debit card tied to the HRA. The card pays eligible expenses directly from the account balance instead of the participant paying first and filing a claim. This document answers the questions that reach the call center most often. Agents may share the card status, the last four digits and the general reason for a decline. They never read, request or record the full card number.

## What the card does

The card draws from the current HRA balance. It works at merchants with a healthcare or insurance merchant category, such as insurance carriers, pharmacies, doctors and hospitals. It does not work at grocery or general retail stores unless the store's system flags eligible items. Premium payments can be made with the card by phone or on the carrier's website like any other card payment.

## Activation

A new card arrives in a plain envelope within seven to ten business days. Activate it by calling the number on the sticker or in the portal, then set a PIN. The card cannot be used before activation.

## Why a card is declined

The most common reasons, in order:

1. The card was never activated.
2. The balance is lower than the purchase amount. The card does not split a payment; the participant pays another way and files a claim for the eligible part.
3. The merchant is not in an eligible category.
4. The card is blocked because a previous purchase still needs documentation. See the next section.
5. The card has expired or the merchant entered an old expiration date.
6. The billing ZIP entered online does not match the address on file.

Agents look up the account, state the card status and the balance, and explain the likely reason. If the status is blocked, explain how to clear it.

## Substantiation requests and blocked cards

Card purchases are approved at the register, but the plan still has to confirm that each purchase was an eligible expense. Recurring premium payments to the carrier of the participant's enrolled plan are approved automatically. Other purchases may generate a request for documentation. The participant receives up to three notices with a deadline to upload an itemized receipt or statement. If nothing is received, the card is blocked until the participant uploads the document or repays the amount. The card is unblocked within two business days after that.

## Lost or stolen card

Report a lost or stolen card immediately in the portal or by phone. The card is blocked at once and a replacement is mailed within seven to ten business days. Transactions made after the report are not the participant's responsibility. Recurring premium payments set up on the old card must be updated with the carrier when the new card arrives.

## Card and claims together

A participant can use the card for some expenses and file claims for others. The same expense should not be paid with the card and also claimed; the second one is denied as a duplicate. Participants who use premium auto-reimbursement should not also pay that premium with the card. See [KB-ACC-003].

## Card at plan year end

The card keeps working into the new plan year if the sponsor rolls over funds or loads the new annual amount. If the sponsor forfeits unused funds, purchases after December 31 draw only from the new allocation. Prior-year expenses filed during the run-out period must be submitted as claims, not paid with the card.

## What agents may say

State the card status (active, blocked, not issued, expired), the last four digits, the balance and the reason category. Explain the steps to activate, unblock or replace. Route to a benefit advisor when a participant disputes a transaction or reports fraud beyond a simple lost card. Cite this document.
