# Comparing Plans, What Matters

Participants usually start with the monthly premium. Premium is one input among several. This document lists the factors a neutral comparison should show, in the order that matters most for total cost and access to care, and explains how agents present a comparison without recommending a plan.

## Total yearly cost, not premium alone

A useful comparison shows what drives a participant's yearly cost:

- Premium multiplied by twelve.
- The deductible, if the participant is likely to use enough care to reach it.
- Copays and coinsurance for the visits and services the participant expects.
- Prescription costs, which depend on the formulary tier of each drug and the pharmacy used.
- The maximum out-of-pocket amount as the worst-case ceiling for medical services.

A $0 premium plan with a high maximum out-of-pocket can cost more in a bad year than a plan with a premium and a lower ceiling. The agent shows both numbers and lets the participant weigh them. Agents never estimate a participant's personal total; they show the plan facts.

## Provider network

Whether a participant's doctors are in network is often the deciding factor.

- HMO plans require in-network care and usually a referral to see a specialist.
- PPO plans cover out-of-network care at a higher cost share and do not require referrals.
- Medigap policies have no network; any provider who accepts Medicare accepts them.

Agents cannot confirm whether a specific doctor is in a plan's network. The plan records in the marketplace tools do not contain provider directories. The correct response is to name the network type, explain what it means for the participant's stated doctors, and note that the participant can check the carrier's directory and that a licensed benefit advisor verifies the doctor during enrollment.

## Prescription drugs

For each medication, show the formulary tier and note that the tier drives the copay. Tier 1 usually means the lowest copay, often $0 at preferred pharmacies. The plan records include example tiers for a few common drugs only; if a drug is not listed, say the formulary needs to be checked rather than guessing. Ask whether the participant uses mail order or a specific pharmacy, since preferred pharmacies change the price.

## Star ratings

CMS publishes star ratings from 1 to 5 for Medicare Advantage and Part D plans. Medigap policies are not rated. ACA plans carry a separate Marketplace quality rating. Show the rating as one fact in the table, not as a ranking.

## Extra benefits and travel

Dental, vision, hearing, over-the-counter allowances, fitness memberships and transportation benefits appear in many Medicare Advantage plans and rarely in Medigap or Part D. Participants who travel or spend part of the year in another state often look at PPO plans with national networks or at Medigap. Point to the highlight list in the plan record.

## Service area

Plans are approved by county. A participant who moves may lose access to the current plan and gain a Special Enrollment Period. See [KB-MKT-001].

## How to present a comparison

- Use the same fields for every plan: premium, deductible, maximum out-of-pocket, network type, drug tier for the participant's medications, star rating, dental and vision, key highlights.
- Include the participant's current plan in the table so the difference is visible.
- Filter on the participant's stated priorities and say that you did so. Do not add your own ranking.
- Do not use the words best, recommend, should or right for you. Keep the neutral premium order; do not sort in a way that implies a winner.
- Cite this document and state the enrollment window with [KB-MKT-001].
- Close by offering a licensed benefit advisor who can review the comparison, confirm the doctors and complete an enrollment. See [KB-UNI-001].
